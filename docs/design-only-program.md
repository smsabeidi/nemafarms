# NemaFarms — the $230,000 website design program

22 September 2026. This document supersedes the budget, scope and schedule in website-strategy.md. Current commission: WEBSITE DESIGN ONLY. No engineering, CMS setup, integration, deployment or production operations are included. Prototypes may use code to demonstrate design; they are not production software. All fees below are proposed design allocations, not third-party quotations or an authorization to spend.

## The ambition

Create the defining visual expression of NemaFarms: a Gambian farm whose website feels authored, culturally grounded, commercially confident and unusually beautiful. The identity should be recognizable in a single crop, without the logo. Every screen should reward attention, and every practical task should be understandable.

The design fee buys depth of exploration, exceptional composition, original visual language and exhaustive resolution. It does not buy more decorative sections. The standard is a coherent experience from a 360px phone to a large desktop, including the menu, product details, journal typography, form errors and quiet moments between major images.

Use “June 2026 grade” as a requested level of contemporary craft, not a literal launch deadline or a claim that a particular visual style is an industry standard. Do not promise awards.

An authentic visual language comes from the farm's people, buildings, landscape, tools, crops and materials. Do not invent ethnic motifs, use generalized “African” patterns, fabricate testimonials, or label draft imagery as a real product. Any cultural reference should have a named local source and be reviewed with NemaFarms.

## Creative exploration: three complete routes

Explore all three with real page content. Each route receives a desktop hero, complete homepage rhythm, mobile hero and menu, product-detail excerpt, journal excerpt, color/type sheet and 15–20-second motion study. This makes the choice about a system rather than one attractive opening frame.

### A. The Living Field — recommended lead

Bold typographic editorial design. A large, compact headline above or beside a nearly edge-to-edge photograph. Deep olive, pale sand, warm white and charcoal. Documentary imagery, asymmetric margins, fine rules, small factual captions and occasional italic contrast.

Proposed headline: **Good food. Gambian roots.** This is draft brand copy, not a factual product claim. Supporting copy should say what the farm actually offers once confirmed.

The distinctive device is the relationship between oversized words and open land: type establishes the idea, photography reveals the place, and a small caption anchors reality. Natural vertical scrolling alternates immersive images with quieter reading passages. Product sections have editorial image scales rather than identical card boxes.

Risk: large expressive type can overpower the existing logo. Resolve by testing the actual wordmark, fewer competing type styles and deliberate hierarchy. Mobile must get a composed layout of its own.

### B. The Nema Journal

An intimate farm publication: serif-led headlines, generous margins, photographic diptychs, small chapter numbers and restrained paper tones. Existing script identity becomes a natural masthead. Picture captions, people and long-form stories lead the experience.

Proposed headline: **A farm. A community. A shared tomorrow.** Portraiture and first-person storytelling make it human; product information remains direct and easy to reach.

Risk: literary styling can obscure the product offer or make the business feel like a publication. Resolve by placing the product proposition and enquiry route in the first screen and repeating clear actions at natural points.

### C. Grown With Purpose

Confident graphic simplicity: olive fields, bold sans-serif statements, flat color, cropped product photography and a restrained original illustration family. The composition is more direct and energetic, with strong product silhouettes and concise benefit-led sections.

Proposed headline: **From our farm. For our future.** Product/category contrasts create rhythm; community storytelling provides depth.

Risk: familiar organic-food styling. Resolve through original photography, distinctive typographic alignment and illustrations derived from actual farm objects rather than generic leaves.

## Choose one, then refine deeply

At the first design review, evaluate each route on five questions: Is this recognizably NemaFarms? Is the offer understood? Does it feel distinctive? Does the mobile composition work? Can the same language sustain product, story and contact pages?

Select one route for development. Resolve weaknesses with additional studies inside that route; avoid combining every attractive device from all three. Keep a decision log with accepted and rejected treatments so the final system is coherent.

Explore 6–8 hero compositions within the chosen route: stacked type, split editorial, photographic overlay, framed landscape, portrait/landscape pairing and a typographic opening. Test each with the same content and real photography. Narrow to two, compare on phone and desktop, then choose one.

## Homepage choreography for the leading route

1. **Recognition:** compact wordmark and clear navigation; a short expressive headline and an identifiable farm photograph. Product and supply enquiry are visible without needing an animation to finish.
2. **Orientation:** one plain sentence describing the verified offering. This sentence must survive all expressive headline experiments.
3. **The offer:** three categories if confirmed. One dominant product photograph and two supporting compositions; deliberate asymmetry, consistent information hierarchy.
4. **The people:** a wide-to-close photographic transition into a named, verified story. Give the quote and caption room to breathe.
5. **The practice:** a small sequence of tangible activities, with corresponding images or original line drawings. Each claim earns its place.
6. **The buyer:** a clear wholesale invitation with the important purchasing information nearby.
7. **The wider purpose:** community story, then journal. Future ambitions use future tense and a distinct content label.
8. **The close:** a confident olive footer with a direct contact invitation, practical information and generous wordmark treatment.

No section gets added merely to lengthen the page. Each must introduce a new piece of information or a useful next action. Test a shorter homepage against the full editorial sequence.

## Art-direction system

### Typography

Deliver three considered type pairings during exploration and one final hierarchy. Include hero, display, page title, section title, product title, body, lead paragraph, navigation, captions, labels, buttons, numerical information and form feedback. Test real long titles and small screens. Confirm licensing before final font selection.

Use the current wordmark when available as a proper asset. Any early text-rendered interpretation is explicitly a placeholder. The final system should not rely on a substitute script font to approximate the logo.

### Layout

Specify desktop, tablet and mobile grids, content widths, spacing scale, image ratios, alignment rules, section rhythm and density. Design representative compositions at 1440, 768 and 390px; stress-test 360, 320 and 1920px. Key templates require all three primary widths. Standard informational pages can inherit tested templates with documented behavior.

### Color and material

Keep olive/sand as the center of gravity. Test sage for supporting surfaces and a very limited food-derived accent only if it improves the chosen route. Design functional colors separately from the expressive palette. Texture must remain subtle and must never reduce reading contrast. Provide an accessible color-pairing table.

### Photography

Create a shot-by-shot art-direction brief: subject, framing, light, orientation, copy-safe space, intended template and minimum crop options. Include portraits at eye level, landscape/context, hands at work, products in honest surroundings, tools/materials and dispatch if relevant. Identify 12 essential launch shots and a 30–40-image ideal library.

Design includes contact-sheet selection, crop specifications, grading references, image sequence and production briefing. Hiring crews, travel, shooting and final commercial asset production are separate expenses; do not bury them in this design-only fee. Use existing assets and clearly marked placeholders in the meantime.

### Illustration and iconography

Develop one original small illustration family based on verified farm subjects. Deliver roughly 8–12 editorial illustrations if the chosen route needs them. Use a consistent functional icon set for navigation and controls. Decorative imagery must not be mistaken for certifications or awards.

## Interaction design: every state has an owner

Document default, hover, focus-visible, pressed, disabled, loading, success and error where applicable. Design states in context as well as on a component sheet.

- Header: light/dark surfaces, scrolled state, long navigation labels and mobile menu open/closed.
- Mobile menu: clear hierarchy, contact action, close behavior, focus expectations and return to the invoking control.
- Buttons/links: quiet and prominent variants, multiline labels, keyboard focus and reduced-motion behavior.
- Catalogue: available, seasonal, unavailable, missing image, long description and empty category.
- Product enquiry: selected product, quantity editing, required-field feedback, invalid contact, submitting, success, server failure and duplicate-submit feedback.
- Gallery: thumbnail, selected image, previous/next, close, caption wrapping and keyboard/touch behavior.
- Journal: short/long titles, missing optional media, archive end and empty content.
- FAQs: all-collapsed, one expanded, multiple expanded if permitted, and long answers.
- Utilities: 404, contact alternative, empty state and a restrained notice style.

Only design search, filters, language selectors or consent banners when the agreed content, locales or analytics behavior require them. Exhaustiveness means resolving actual requirements, not inventing features.

## Motion direction

Deliver a short motion identity film plus six annotated studies: hero entrance, navigation/menu, section transition, product interaction, gallery and form feedback. Show desktop, phone and reduced-motion alternatives for the principal sequences.

Default intent: subtle local movement, ordinary scrolling, no blocking opening animation. Suggested starting ranges are 150–250ms for controls and 450–700ms for editorial transitions. Specify timing, easing, start/end poses, trigger, interruption behavior and static fallback. These ranges are design proposals to test, not universal rules.

Investigate one memorable signature gesture, such as an image aperture revealing the farm beneath a typographic frame. Keep it only if it strengthens recognition and does not delay the buyer. A stationary opening must still look complete.

## Copy as a design material

Provide the finished design copy for navigation, hero, section headings, product/enquiry prompts and interaction states. Draft the main editorial pages from verified material and mark unsupported statements for confirmation. Design both a concise and a longer version of key sections before settling content length.

Voice: clear, grounded, specific and proud of place. Avoid generic sustainability language, invented statistics, unverified superlatives and language that makes the community a decorative backdrop. Name people and activities accurately. Use future tense for planned work.

## Design validation

Two rounds of moderated prototype testing with approximately five representative users per round, drawn from the priority buyer groups. Treat findings as qualitative. Test whether people can explain the offer, find a relevant product, understand availability and complete an enquiry. Test brand recall and comprehension without showing explanatory designer notes.

Run a structured accessibility design review: contrast, type size, keyboard/focus behavior, labels, error recovery, touch targets, reading order, zoom/reflow expectations and reduced motion. Annotate intended behavior; visual prototypes cannot certify an implemented website's accessibility or speed.

Use weekly craft reviews and formal gates. Inspect each template at normal viewing scale, not only a zoomed-out artboard. Check short/long content, awkward crops and missing optional data. Compare mobile and desktop side by side to ensure both feel intentional.

## What “finished design” means

The final delivery contains an organized editable design source, a coherent prototype, all core templates, responsive behavior, component and state coverage, final approved interface copy, original design assets, image/crop specifications, motion documentation, accessibility design notes and a decision register. Use Figma or the client's agreed equivalent as the source of truth; no Figma file has been created by this planning work.

Every visible figure, promise and quote has an approved source or remains an explicitly marked placeholder. Every primary action has a designed destination. Every essential mobile screen is composed deliberately. No critical interaction is left for an engineer to invent. Any remaining uncertainty is named and assigned rather than hidden in the visual design.
